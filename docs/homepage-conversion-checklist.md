## What Repeats Across Strong Homepages

1. The hero has one job.
   It explains the offer fast and points to one primary action.

2. The headline is short.
   Usually one bold promise or one bold category statement.

3. The support copy is shorter than the headline.
   Not a paragraph stack.

4. There is a visible artifact above the fold.
   Product UI, deliverable preview, video frame, or portfolio proof.

5. The CTA is high-contrast and easy to spot.
   One dominant button. Secondary action is visually quieter.

6. The page reduces uncertainty fast.
   Review badges, proof, sample work, categories, or trust chips appear early.

7. The layout is not crowded.
   Plenty of whitespace, one clear reading path, limited competing card styles.

8. The hero is not text-only.
   The right side usually carries visual weight.

## Research Signals To Use

1. Users scan, especially down the left side first.
   Use strong keywords and value language early in the headline and first supporting line.

2. Busy layouts hurt first impressions.
   Crowded grids, low whitespace, and stock-looking imagery reduce perceived professionalism and make sections harder to distinguish.

3. Fewer visible fields reduce effort.
   Even when steps remain, reducing the number of fields users must consider lowers perceived complexity.

4. Mobile speed and element count matter.
   More page elements and slower loads correlate with lower mobile conversion and higher bounce.

## Recommended Homepage Template

### Navbar

- Logo at left
- 3-5 nav items max
- 1 CTA at right
- CTA label: 2-4 words

### Hero Structure

Desktop layout:

- Left: headline, subhead, CTA, proof, short benefit list
- Right: visual artifact with optional floating form

Mobile layout:

- Headline
- Subhead
- Primary CTA
- Proof chips
- Visual artifact
- Quick-start form

### Hero Word Budget

Use these as limits, not goals:

- Eyebrow: 2-5 words
- H1: 6-12 words
- Subhead: 12-22 words
- Primary CTA: 2-4 words
- Secondary CTA: 3-6 words
- Proof chips: 3 chips, 2-5 words each
- Benefit bullets: 3 bullets, 3-6 words each
- Form intro: 6-12 words

Ideal above-the-fold copy load:

- 35-55 words total excluding nav, form labels, and proof chips

If the hero needs more than that to make sense, the offer is still too muddy.

## Eye Flow Template

Design the above-the-fold scan path like this:

1. Eyebrow: category + niche
2. H1: outcome or diagnosis
3. Subhead: how it works in one sentence
4. Primary CTA
5. Proof chips
6. Visual artifact
7. Optional floating form or quick-start card

Avoid making users choose between two equally loud actions.

## Behavior Elements To Use

1. Dominant primary CTA
   One button that visually wins.

2. Secondary CTA as a text link or low-emphasis button
   Use for proof-first visitors.

3. Deliverable preview
   For SyncWorkflow, this should be a cropped sample audit preview, not just descriptive copy.

4. Low-friction form
   Keep homepage capture to 2 fields max.

5. Proof chips
   Use honest trust reducers:
   - `2 fields to start`
   - `Private audit for small law firms`
   - `See sample findings first`

6. Risk-reduction microcopy
   Example: `Private review. No long intake form up front.`

7. Sticky mobile CTA
   Keep one obvious action available without making the screen feel trapped.

8. Visual hierarchy
   Big H1, smaller subhead, tiny proof text, strong CTA, large artifact.

9. Whitespace around the hero
   Avoid stacking too many bordered cards in the first screen.

## SyncWorkflow Homepage Checklist

### Hero

- [ ] Can a small-law-firm visitor tell what the offer is in 5 seconds?
- [ ] Is the H1 12 words or fewer?
- [ ] Is the subhead 22 words or fewer?
- [ ] Is there exactly 1 dominant CTA?
- [ ] Is the secondary CTA visually quieter?
- [ ] Is there a real audit artifact visible above the fold?
- [ ] Are there only 3 proof chips max?
- [ ] Are there only 3 benefit bullets max?
- [ ] Is the hero readable without scrolling on a laptop?
- [ ] Does the hero avoid large blocks of founder biography text?

### Form

- [ ] Are only 2 fields visible by default?
- [ ] Is the submit button large and obvious?
- [ ] Does the form explain the next step in one short line?
- [ ] Are optional fields hidden until requested?

### Visual Design

- [ ] Does the logo look intentional rather than placeholder?
- [ ] Does the hero have one clear visual focal point?
- [ ] Is the visual artifact large enough to read at a glance?
- [ ] Is whitespace separating sections clearly?
- [ ] Are there no more than 2 major card styles above the fold?

### Messaging

- [ ] Does the copy lead with the user outcome, not the founder story?
- [ ] Does the page avoid agency-generic phrases?
- [ ] Are the benefits framed as user-visible improvements?
- [ ] Is the trust language honest and specific?

### Mobile

- [ ] Is the first action obvious without scrolling too far?
- [ ] Are tap targets at least 44x44 px?
- [ ] Does the hero keep its key content in a clean vertical stack?
- [ ] Is the artifact still visible and useful on mobile?
- [ ] Is the mobile page light enough to load quickly?

## What SyncWorkflow Still Needs Most

1. A tighter, cropped audit preview in the hero
   The full-page screenshot is useful, but a zoomed-in crop of the review is stronger.

2. Less visual competition above the fold
   The first screen should feel like one composed story, not multiple stacked modules.

3. Shorter copy everywhere
   Good meaning, fewer words.

4. Better visual emphasis on the audit artifact
   The deliverable should feel like the star.

5. A more premium logo and type rhythm
   The current mark is better than the placeholder, but the site still needs stronger visual identity.

## Recommended Next Design Pass

1. Crop the audit preview to one strong review panel, not the whole page.
2. Simplify the hero to:
   - eyebrow
   - H1
   - one-line subhead
   - one CTA
   - one quieter proof link
   - 3 proof chips
   - 3 short benefit bullets
   - visual artifact
   - 2-field form
3. Remove one extra content block from the first 800 px of the page.
4. Strengthen typography contrast and spacing before adding more copy.
